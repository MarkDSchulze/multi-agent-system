MULTI-AGENT BUSINESS OPERATIONS SYSTEM
Design Notes
Author: Mark Schulze

PURPOSE
Demonstrate how specialized AI agents can coordinate quoting,
inventory analysis, replenishment validation, and order processing.

AGENT RESPONSIBILITIES
- Orchestrator: Coordinate specialist agents.
- Quote Agent: Retrieve historical examples and estimate item prices.
- Inventory Management Agent: Inspect inventory, supplier timing,
  available cash, and financial reports.
- Ordering Agent: Validate complete orders and record transactions.

BUSINESS RULES
- Resolve customer product names using catalog names and aliases.
- Reject the complete order if any product is unsupported.
- Check stock and calculate required replenishment.
- Reject replenishment that would arrive after the delivery deadline.
- Confirm available cash before purchasing replenishment.
- Apply the highest qualifying order-level quantity discount.
- Generate the customer response from the recorded ordering result.

SUPPLIER LEAD TIMES
Up to 10 units: same day
11–100 units: 1 calendar day
101–1,000 units: 4 calendar days
More than 1,000 units: 7 calendar days

DISCOUNT TIERS
500+ units: 5%
1,000+ units: 10%
5,000+ units: 12%
10,000+ units: 15%

DATA
Historical requests and quotes are paired by source-row position.
Both quote_requests.csv and quotes.csv are required for initialization.
quote_requests_sample.csv supplies the simulation requests.

SIMULATION RESULTS
20 requests: 8 fulfilled and 12 rejected.
Rejections: 5 unsupported-product cases and 7 delivery-timing cases.
These saved outcomes do not establish an automated test pass rate.

DESIGN TRADEOFFS
Deterministic tools control final pricing and fulfillment decisions.
Historical quotes provide context rather than authoritative prices.
Complete-order rejection simplifies processing but excludes backorders
and partial fulfillment.

LIMITATIONS
The ledger books purchases and sales on the request date rather than
modeling physical arrival and shipment events.
Product aliases do not fully validate attributes or packaging units.
Current writes are separate transactions, so complete-order validation
does not guarantee database-level atomicity.
Repeated tool calls and concurrent requests need stronger safeguards.

NEXT STEPS
Add executable business-rule tests, atomic order writes, duplicate-order
protection, explicit quantity units, and structured operational logging.
